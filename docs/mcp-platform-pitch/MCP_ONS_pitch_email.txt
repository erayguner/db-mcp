Subject: A safe first step into the agent era: an MCP server for ONS

OFFICIAL · For discussion

Dear [Name],

The way data is consumed is shifting. A new generation of AI "agents" is moving
into everyday use across government, and these agents increasingly expect to reach
trusted data directly, machine to machine — not through a person navigating a
portal. I'd like your support to take a small, deliberate first step that places
ONS at the front of that shift, safely.

The proposal is to stand up a proof-of-concept Model Context Protocol (MCP) server.
MCP is an emerging open standard — already adopted across the major AI providers —
that gives an AI agent a single, governed way to discover, query and correctly cite
our data. In plain terms, it is a secure "front door" through which approved agents
reach approved data, with every request logged.

WHY THIS IS A SAFE BET, NOT A GAMBLE

  - No model lock-in. MCP is not tied to any one AI model or vendor. It works with
    whatever models ONS, departments or partners choose — today, and as they change.

  - Builds on what we have. It sits on top of our existing platform and governance,
    reusing — not replacing — current APIs and controls.

  - Public data first. The proof of concept uses only already-published data, so
    there is no new data risk. It is low-cost, time-boxed and fully reversible.

WHY MCP — AND WHY IT LASTS

Some consumers already automate retrieval — scraping our published outputs or
hard-wiring to fixed endpoints. That breaks the moment a format changes, and it
pulls numbers without their meaning, so an agent can easily misread or misquote
them. MCP gives an agent what a feed cannot: it discovers what exists and what it
means as it asks; every figure arrives with its definition, provenance and
citation; and one governed, audited route replaces dozens of brittle integrations.
The data is already being pulled — this brings that demand into a controlled
channel rather than leaving it on the open web.

And it is built to last. MCP is an open, vendor-neutral standard, so it is not a
bet on any one model or supplier — it adapts as they change. As AI agents become
the normal way work gets done, the route to data is shifting from screens and
one-off integrations to an agent-native standard. Building to it now is additive
and low-regret: it layers onto our existing platform, and every dataset we expose
becomes reusable by every future agent and department — not something we retrofit
later.

ALREADY HAPPENING IN GOVERNMENT

  - DBT has connected its AI assistant to live CRM data through an MCP server on
    its existing GOV.UK sign-on, with answers cited back to source.
  - India's national statistics office has launched an MCP server over its
    official statistics (~22 datasets) for AI tools.

We would be following a proven path — and GDS guidance already recommends MCP for
connecting AI to government data.

WHAT IT UNLOCKS

ONS could be the first to give internal teams and other government departments a
direct, governed route for their AI agents — a markedly better experience than
today's portals and manual requests, and a reusable capability the wider public
sector can build on. It reinforces our role as the trusted, AI-ready source of
data and insight.

WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ONS

There is a sharper reason for ONS to act. In an AI-mediated world, people will
increasingly ask assistants "what is inflation?" or "how has unemployment changed?"
— and they will get an answer whether or not it is correct. An MCP server lets
those assistants return ONS's authoritative, cited figure rather than a
plausible-sounding guess — protecting the integrity of official statistics and our
role as the UK's trusted source. It also aligns squarely with the government's AI
agenda, including the AI Playbook for Government and the drive to treat
public-sector data as a strategic national asset.
